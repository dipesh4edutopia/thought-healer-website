# Why Does My Heart Race When I'm Anxious?

If your heart suddenly starts beating fast when you're worried, frightened, or under stress, anxiety may be activating your body's **fight-or-flight response**. When the brain detects a possible threat, the body releases stress hormones such as adrenaline. These changes can increase your heart rate and make your heartbeat feel stronger or more noticeable.

**However, a racing heart is not always caused by anxiety.** Heart palpitations can have many possible causes, including caffeine, exercise, fever, medications, dehydration, and heart-rhythm problems. New, severe, persistent, recurrent, or concerning symptoms should be medically evaluated rather than automatically attributed to anxiety.

> **Featured-snippet answer:** Anxiety can make your heart race because it activates the body's fight-or-flight response. Stress hormones such as adrenaline increase heart activity, preparing you to respond to a perceived threat. You may also become more aware of your heartbeat, making it feel especially strong or noticeable.

## Why Does Anxiety Make Your Heart Race?

Your heart does not speed up simply because you are "imagining" something stressful. **The physical response is real.**

When you feel anxious, your brain may interpret a situation as threatening—even when the threat is emotional rather than physical.

For example, imagine that you are about to give an important presentation. You may think:

_“What if I make a mistake?”_

Your brain responds to that perceived threat by activating the body's stress-response system. Stress hormones, including adrenaline, are released. Your heart may then beat faster and more forcefully, while other parts of the body become more alert and ready to respond.

In simple terms:

**Perceived threat → stress response → adrenaline → increased heart activity → racing or pounding heartbeat**

This response is useful when you genuinely need to react quickly. The same system can also become activated by worry, fear, anticipation, or emotional distress.

That is why you can experience a fast heartbeat even when you are sitting safely at home.

## What Does an Anxiety-Related Racing Heart Feel Like?

Anxiety and racing heart sensations can vary from person to person.

You might notice:

- A fast heartbeat
    
- A pounding heartbeat
    
- A heartbeat that feels unusually strong
    
- Fluttering in your chest
    
- A sensation of skipped or extra beats
    
- Your heartbeat in your chest, throat, or neck
    
- Suddenly becoming very aware of your heartbeat
    

These sensations are often described as **heart palpitations**. Palpitations are a symptom or sensation rather than a diagnosis, and the heart rhythm can be normal or abnormal when they occur.

Some people notice their heart speeding up gradually as worry builds. Others may suddenly become aware of their heartbeat during an intense period of anxiety.

Importantly, **not every palpitation is caused by anxiety**. Stress is one possible trigger among several.

## Why Does My Heart Feel Like It's Pounding Out of My Chest?

When anxiety activates the stress response, your heart may beat faster and more forcefully.

At the same time, anxiety can make you pay closer attention to what is happening inside your body.

For example, you might normally not notice your heartbeat. But after becoming anxious, you may suddenly focus on every beat.

This can make a normal physiological change feel much more dramatic.

The sensation is **not imaginary**. Your heartbeat really can become faster, stronger, or more noticeable. The additional attention you give it may simply make the sensation stand out even more. Anxiety itself can cause a racing or more noticeable heartbeat.

---

## Can Anxiety Cause Heart Palpitations?

**Yes. Anxiety and stress can contribute to heart palpitations.**

Heart palpitations are sensations that your heart is:

- Racing
    
- Pounding
    
- Fluttering
    
- Beating unusually strongly
    
- Skipping or adding a beat
    

They can occur in the chest, throat, or neck.

Anxiety, fear, and panic are recognized triggers of palpitations. However, palpitations can also occur for other reasons, including exercise, caffeine, fever, some medications, stimulant substances, thyroid problems, and abnormal heart rhythms.

So if you experience anxiety heart palpitations, it is reasonable to consider anxiety as one possible explanation—but not to assume it is automatically the cause.

## What Other Physical Symptoms Can Anxiety Cause?

Anxiety can affect much more than your thoughts and emotions.

Possible physical symptoms include:

- Sweating
    
- Trembling or shaking
    
- Muscle tension
    
- Dizziness or light-headedness
    
- Shortness of breath
    
- Stomach discomfort
    
- Nausea
    
- Feeling unusually hot
    
- Feeling cold or chilled
    
- Fatigue
    
- A faster, stronger, or more noticeable heartbeat
    

These symptoms can occur because the body's stress-response system changes activity throughout the body.

However, these symptoms are **not specific to anxiety**. Other physical or medical conditions can cause similar symptoms, which is one reason self-diagnosis can be unreliable.

## How Long Does an Anxiety-Related Racing Heart Last?

There is no single amount of time that an anxiety-related racing heart should last.

It may depend on:

- How intense the anxiety is
    
- What triggered it
    
- Whether the stressful situation is still happening
    
- How quickly your body settles after the perceived threat
    
- Individual differences in how people respond to stress
    

As the stress response decreases, the heart rate may gradually return toward its usual level.

A racing heart that **continues for a prolonged period, keeps returning, occurs without an obvious trigger, or is becoming more frequent** deserves medical attention.

Likewise, if you have other concerning symptoms, do not assume that the duration alone proves the symptoms are anxiety-related.

# How Can I Calm My Racing Heart When I'm Anxious?

If anxiety appears to be contributing to the sensation, a few low-risk strategies may help your body move out of a heightened state.

### 1. Slow Your Breathing

Try breathing slowly and comfortably rather than taking very large breaths or holding your breath for long periods.

The goal is not to force your heart rate down immediately. Instead, give your body a chance to settle.

You might gently breathe in and then allow a slow, relaxed exhale.

If focusing on breathing makes you more uncomfortable, stop and try another grounding technique.

### 2. Ground Yourself in the Present

Anxiety can pull your attention toward frightening thoughts about what might happen.

Instead, deliberately notice your surroundings.

You could identify:

- Things you can see
    
- Sounds you can hear
    
- The feeling of your feet against the floor
    
- The chair supporting your body
    
- Objects around you
    

Grounding can shift attention away from repeatedly analyzing your heartbeat.

### 3. Relax Muscle Tension

Anxiety often causes people to tense their muscles without realizing it.

Try consciously relaxing:

- Your jaw
    
- Shoulders
    
- Hands
    
- Forehead
    
- Neck
    

You do not need to force your body to relax. Simply notice tension and allow it to soften.

### 4. Sit Somewhere Comfortable

If you feel overwhelmed, dizzy, or light-headed, sit down somewhere safe and comfortable.

Give yourself a moment before deciding what to do next.

### 5. Remind Yourself What May Be Happening

You might tell yourself:

> **“My body may be responding to stress. I can give it some time to settle.”**

This can be a useful way to avoid immediately interpreting every heartbeat as evidence that something terrible is happening.

But remember: **this statement is not proof that every racing heartbeat is harmless.** If your symptoms are new, severe, unusual, persistent, or concerning, seek appropriate medical advice.

### 6. Consider Reducing Stimulants

Caffeine can contribute to fast heart rate, anxiety, and palpitations in some people. Other stimulants and certain medications can also affect heart rate.

If you notice that coffee, energy drinks, nicotine, or another stimulant consistently makes these sensations worse, reducing exposure may help.

### 7. Avoid Repeatedly Checking Your Pulse

Constantly checking your pulse can keep your attention locked onto your heartbeat.

For some people, this creates a cycle:

**Notice heartbeat → check pulse → worry about the result → become more anxious → notice heartbeat even more**

Once a serious cause has been appropriately evaluated, repeatedly monitoring every heartbeat may increase stress rather than provide lasting reassurance.

# Why Does Anxiety Make Me Notice My Heartbeat More?

Anxiety can create a feedback loop between physical sensations, attention, and fear.

Consider this example:

**Anxiety increases → heartbeat becomes more noticeable → you focus on your heartbeat → you wonder whether something is wrong → anxiety increases → heartbeat feels even stronger.**

This does not mean the sensation is "all in your head."

Your heart may genuinely be beating faster or more forcefully. Anxiety can simply make you much more aware of that change.

Understanding this feedback loop can sometimes make the sensation less frightening.

---

# Can Worry About My Heart Make My Heart Beat Faster?

**Yes, it can.**

Imagine that you notice your heart beating quickly and immediately think:

_“Something is seriously wrong with my heart.”_

That thought may increase fear.

The increased fear can activate the body's stress response further, potentially making your heart beat faster or feel stronger.

The cycle may look like this:

**Fast heartbeat → “Something is wrong” → fear → increased stress response → stronger heartbeat → more fear**

Learning to recognize this pattern can help you respond to the sensation without immediately escalating the worry.

But recognizing the anxiety cycle should **not** be used to dismiss new or concerning physical symptoms.

---

# Is a Racing Heart From Anxiety Dangerous?

A faster heartbeat can be a normal part of the body's response to stress and anxiety. Anxiety is a recognized cause of rapid or noticeable heartbeats.

However, **you cannot determine the cause of a racing heart from the sensation alone**.

Other possible causes include:

- Exercise
    
- Caffeine
    
- Dehydration
    
- Fever or illness
    
- Certain medications
    
- Stimulants
    
- Thyroid problems
    
- Abnormal heart rhythms
    
- Other medical conditions
    

This is why the safest approach is neither _“It's definitely anxiety”_ nor _“A racing heart means something is seriously wrong.”_

Instead, consider the full pattern of symptoms and seek medical evaluation when appropriate.

# Anxiety vs. a Possible Heart Problem

Certain patterns may make an anxiety response seem more likely, while other patterns are reasons to seek medical evaluation.

|More consistent with an anxiety response|Reasons to seek medical evaluation|
|---|---|
|Occurs during obvious stress or fear|Happens repeatedly without an obvious trigger|
|Occurs alongside familiar anxiety symptoms|Symptoms are new or unusual for you|
|Improves as anxiety settles|Persists or repeatedly returns|
|Similar to previous anxiety episodes|Feels substantially different from previous episodes|
|No concerning additional symptoms|Chest pain, fainting, severe breathing difficulty, or other alarming symptoms|

**Important:** This table cannot determine the cause of your symptoms. Anxiety and medical conditions can overlap, and some heart problems can cause symptoms that resemble anxiety. A healthcare professional can assess your history, symptoms, and whether testing is appropriate.

# When Should I Seek Urgent Medical Help?

Seek **urgent medical attention or your local emergency services** if a racing or irregular heartbeat occurs with symptoms such as:

- New or severe chest pain or discomfort
    
- Severe difficulty breathing
    
- Fainting or loss of consciousness
    
- Severe dizziness or light-headedness
    
- Significant weakness
    
- Other sudden or alarming symptoms
    

Heart palpitations accompanied by chest pain, fainting, severe shortness of breath, or severe dizziness warrant emergency assessment.

You do not need to prove that your symptoms are caused by a heart problem before seeking help.

If you are unsure whether what you are experiencing is anxiety or a medical problem, it is appropriate to seek medical advice.

---

# When Should I Talk to a Doctor About a Racing Heart?

Consider talking with a healthcare professional if:

- You repeatedly experience racing or pounding heartbeats
    
- Episodes occur without an obvious trigger
    
- The sensation persists or is becoming more frequent
    
- Your heartbeat feels irregular
    
- Episodes are different from what you have experienced before
    
- Symptoms interfere with everyday activities
    
- You are concerned that an underlying physical condition may be involved
    

A healthcare professional can look at the timing and pattern of your symptoms, medical history, medications and other possible factors, and decide whether further evaluation is needed.

---

# When Should I Talk to a Mental-Health Professional?

If anxiety is happening frequently, is difficult to control, or is interfering with your work, relationships, sleep, or everyday activities, professional support may be useful.

You might speak with a:

- Therapist
    
- Psychologist
    
- Psychiatrist
    
- Primary-care professional
    

Anxiety disorders are diagnosed by considering symptoms, medical history, and other possible explanations. Treatment can include evidence-based psychological therapies and, when appropriate, other professional treatment options.

Getting help does not mean that your physical symptoms are "just psychological." Anxiety can produce genuine physical changes, including a rapid or pounding heartbeat.

---

# Can Anxiety Cause Long-Term Heart Problems?

Short-term increases in heart rate are a normal part of the body's stress response.

The relationship between anxiety, chronic stress, and cardiovascular health is more complicated than saying either _“anxiety causes heart disease”_ or _“anxiety has nothing to do with heart health.”_

Long-term cardiovascular health is influenced by many factors, including existing medical conditions, lifestyle, genetics, and other forms of stress and behavior.

For that reason, it is better not to use a single symptom such as a racing heartbeat to predict your long-term heart health.

If you have ongoing anxiety or concerns about your cardiovascular health, discuss them with a healthcare professional.

---

# Does Anxiety Increase Heart Rate Even When I'm Not Feeling Panicked?

Yes. Anxiety does not always feel like a dramatic panic attack.

Ongoing worry or stress can also be associated with physical changes such as a faster or more noticeable heartbeat. The NHS lists faster, irregular, or more noticeable heartbeats among possible physical symptoms of anxiety.

You might notice this during:

- Chronic stress
    
- Anticipation of an important event
    
- Worry about the future
    
- Fear
    
- Emotional distress
    
- Difficult conversations or situations
    

Still, an unexplained fast heart rate should not automatically be labeled as anxiety. If the symptom is new, persistent, recurrent, or concerning, medical evaluation may be appropriate.

# Frequently Asked Questions

## 1. Why does my heart race when I'm anxious?

Anxiety can activate the body's fight-or-flight response. Stress hormones such as adrenaline can increase heart activity, causing your heart to beat faster or more forcefully. You may also become more aware of your heartbeat.

## 2. Can anxiety cause a fast heart rate?

**Yes.** Anxiety and fear can activate the body's stress response and cause a faster heartbeat. However, other conditions and substances can also increase heart rate, so a fast heartbeat should not automatically be assumed to be anxiety.

## 3. Can anxiety cause heart palpitations?

**Yes.** Anxiety and stress are common triggers of palpitations, which may feel like racing, pounding, fluttering, or skipped beats. Palpitations can also have other causes.

## 4. How do I calm my racing heart from anxiety?

Try slow, comfortable breathing, grounding yourself in your surroundings, relaxing tense muscles, and sitting somewhere comfortable. Reducing stimulants such as caffeine may also help if they trigger your symptoms. These techniques do not prove that a racing heartbeat is caused by anxiety.

## 5. How long does an anxiety-related racing heart last?

There is no universal duration. It can vary depending on the intensity and duration of the anxiety and individual factors. The heart rate may gradually settle as the stress response decreases. Persistent or recurrent episodes should be discussed with a healthcare professional.

## 6. Why can I feel my heartbeat when I'm anxious?

Anxiety can increase heart activity while also making you more focused on physical sensations. As a result, your heartbeat may feel unusually noticeable in your chest, throat, or neck.

## 7. Can anxiety make my heart pound?

**Yes.** Anxiety can cause a stronger or more noticeable heartbeat as part of the body's stress response. However, pounding heartbeats can have causes other than anxiety.

## 8. How do I know if my racing heart is caused by anxiety?

There is no reliable way to determine the cause based only on how the sensation feels. A pattern involving stress or fear and improvement as anxiety settles may be consistent with anxiety, but recurring, unexplained, persistent, unusual, or concerning symptoms should be medically assessed.

## 9. When should I worry about a racing heart?

Seek urgent medical help when a racing or irregular heartbeat occurs with symptoms such as chest pain, severe difficulty breathing, fainting, or severe dizziness. For recurring or unexplained episodes without emergency symptoms, arrange a medical evaluation.

## 10. Can anxiety cause long-term heart problems?

Anxiety can cause short-term changes in heart rate through the body's stress response. The relationship between ongoing anxiety, chronic stress, and long-term cardiovascular health is complex, so anxiety alone should not be used to determine someone's heart-disease risk. Discuss ongoing concerns with a healthcare professional.

# The Bottom Line

**Why does my heart race when I'm anxious?** Usually, it is because anxiety can activate the body's fight-or-flight response. Stress hormones increase heart activity, and heightened attention can make the heartbeat feel even more noticeable.

For many people, understanding this connection can make the sensation less frightening.

But a racing heart should not automatically be blamed on anxiety. Palpitations and fast heart rates have many possible causes. If symptoms are new, persistent, recurrent, unusual, severe, or accompanied by chest pain, fainting, severe breathing difficulty, severe dizziness, or other alarming symptoms, seek appropriate medical care.

The goal is not to fear every heartbeat—or to ignore one. **It is to understand the body's stress response while knowing when a symptom deserves professional evaluation.**

# What Are the Symptoms of Depression? Signs You Should Know

Depression can affect much more than your mood. It can change the way you **think, feel, behave, sleep, eat, work, and interact with other people**.

Common symptoms include persistent sadness or emptiness, loss of interest in things you once enjoyed, hopelessness, low energy, changes in sleep or appetite, difficulty concentrating, feelings of guilt or worthlessness, and physical aches or digestive problems. Some people also experience irritability, withdrawal, or feeling emotionally numb.

Not everyone with depression experiences the same symptoms, and having one or two of these signs does not necessarily mean you have depression. Symptoms can have other explanations, including stress, grief, anxiety, medications, or physical health conditions. A qualified healthcare professional can help determine what may be happening.

## What Is Depression?

Depression is more than feeling sad for a few days.

Everyone experiences periods of sadness, disappointment, stress, or low mood. These feelings are a normal part of life and may improve as circumstances change.

Depression is different because symptoms can be more persistent and can significantly affect everyday life. Depression may interfere with work, school, relationships, sleep, eating, self-care, and other normal activities.

Depression can affect anyone, and it does not always look the same from one person to another.

Some people may feel deeply sad. Others may mainly feel **empty, numb, exhausted, irritable, disconnected, or unable to enjoy anything**.

---

# What Are the Main Symptoms of Depression?

Depression symptoms can be grouped into several areas: emotional, cognitive, physical, behavioral, and social.

You do not have to experience every symptom.

According to the National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH), common symptoms include changes in mood, interest, energy, concentration, sleep, appetite, physical wellbeing, and thoughts about death or suicide.

## 1. Persistent Sadness or Low Mood

One of the most recognizable symptoms of depression is feeling persistently sad, low, or empty.

You might feel:

- Sad most of the time
    
- Empty or emotionally numb
    
- Tearful
    
- Discouraged
    
- Hopeless
    
- Irritable
    
- Frustrated
    
- Emotionally overwhelmed
    

However, depression does not always feel like sadness.

Some people describe it as feeling like they have **lost their emotional connection to life**.

You might think:

> "I don't necessarily feel sad. I just don't feel like myself anymore."

That can also be a sign that something deserves attention.

---

# 2. Losing Interest or Pleasure

Another major symptom of depression is losing interest in activities you normally enjoy.

You may stop getting pleasure from:

- Hobbies
    
- Exercise
    
- Music
    
- Watching movies
    
- Spending time with friends
    
- Family activities
    
- Social events
    
- Work or studies
    
- Sex or intimacy
    
- Food
    
- Things you previously looked forward to
    

This is sometimes described as **loss of interest or pleasure**.

For example, you may continue playing your favorite game or meeting your friends, but it no longer feels enjoyable.

You might wonder:

**"Why don't I enjoy the things I used to love?"**

A significant change in your ability to experience pleasure can be an important depression symptom.

---

# 3. Feeling Hopeless or Helpless

Depression can affect how you see the future.

You may feel:

- Hopeless
    
- Helpless
    
- Pessimistic
    
- Like nothing will improve
    
- Like your problems have no solution
    
- Like trying is pointless
    

These thoughts can become increasingly convincing when someone is depressed.

But feeling hopeless does not mean that your future is actually hopeless.

It is a symptom that can occur with depression—not proof of what your future will be.

---

# 4. Feeling Worthless or Excessively Guilty

Depression can also change the way you see yourself.

You might experience thoughts such as:

- "I'm not good enough."
    
- "Everything is my fault."
    
- "I'm a failure."
    
- "I'm a burden."
    
- "I should be doing more."
    
- "I don't deserve good things."
    

NIMH identifies feelings of guilt, worthlessness, or helplessness as common symptoms of depression.

Occasional guilt is normal. Depression-related guilt can be much more persistent or disproportionate to what actually happened.

---

# 5. Low Energy and Constant Fatigue

Feeling tired is another common symptom.

Depression-related fatigue may make even simple activities feel difficult.

You might:

- Feel exhausted after sleeping
    
- Struggle to get out of bed
    
- Have very little motivation
    
- Feel physically slowed down
    
- Find routine tasks unusually difficult
    
- Need more effort to complete ordinary responsibilities
    

You may even think:

**"I know I need to do this, but I just can't make myself start."**

Fatigue can occur for many reasons, though, including poor sleep, medical conditions, medications, stress, and other mental-health problems. Persistent fatigue should not automatically be assumed to be depression.

---

# 6. Changes in Sleep

Depression can affect sleep in different ways.

Some people experience **insomnia**, while others sleep much more than usual.

You may notice:

- Difficulty falling asleep
    
- Waking frequently during the night
    
- Waking unusually early
    
- Sleeping longer than usual
    
- Feeling unrefreshed after sleep
    
- Spending excessive time in bed
    

Changes in sleep can also occur with anxiety, stress, physical illness, medications, and other conditions.

What matters is noticing whether your sleep has changed significantly from your usual pattern.

---

# 7. Changes in Appetite or Weight

Depression can change your relationship with food.

Some people lose their appetite, while others eat more than usual.

Possible signs include:

- Eating much less
    
- Eating much more
    
- Losing interest in food
    
- Increased cravings or comfort eating
    
- Unplanned weight loss
    
- Unplanned weight gain
    

These changes may occur because depression affects appetite, energy, routines, or emotional wellbeing.

Significant or unexplained weight changes should also be discussed with a healthcare professional because they can have physical causes unrelated to depression.

---

# 8. Difficulty Concentrating

Depression can affect your ability to think clearly.

You may find it harder to:

- Concentrate
    
- Remember information
    
- Read
    
- Follow conversations
    
- Make decisions
    
- Organize tasks
    
- Complete work
    
- Stay focused
    

For example, you might sit down to complete a simple task and realize that you have been staring at the same screen for 20 minutes.

This does not mean you are lazy or incapable.

Difficulty concentrating can be part of depression, although it can also occur with anxiety, sleep deprivation, stress, ADHD, medications, and other conditions.

---

# 9. Irritability and Anger

Depression does not always look like sadness.

For some people, particularly some men, depression may show up as:

- Irritability
    
- Anger
    
- Frustration
    
- Restlessness
    
- Being unusually impatient
    
- Becoming easily upset
    

You may notice that things that normally would not bother you suddenly feel overwhelming.

NIMH notes that increased anger, irritability, frustration, or restlessness can occur alongside depression.

---

# 10. Withdrawing From Friends and Family

Depression can make social interaction feel exhausting.

You might:

- Stop replying to messages
    
- Cancel plans
    
- Avoid phone calls
    
- Spend more time alone
    
- Stop attending social events
    
- Avoid family members
    
- Lose interest in hobbies you once shared with others
    

Sometimes withdrawal happens because you do not have the energy to interact.

Other times, you may feel like you are a burden or that nobody would understand.

Social withdrawal is one reason depression can become difficult to recognize from the outside.

---

# 11. Losing Motivation

Depression can make ordinary tasks feel disproportionately difficult.

You might struggle to:

- Get out of bed
    
- Shower
    
- Cook
    
- Clean
    
- Go to work or school
    
- Exercise
    
- Answer messages
    
- Complete assignments
    
- Take care of responsibilities
    

This can create a frustrating cycle:

**Low mood → low motivation → fewer activities → more isolation → lower mood**

A person experiencing this may incorrectly conclude that they are simply lazy.

Depression can affect motivation and the ability to carry out normal activities.

---

# 12. Physical Symptoms of Depression

Depression can affect the body as well as the mind.

Possible physical symptoms include:

- Fatigue
    
- Body aches
    
- Headaches
    
- Muscle or other physical discomfort
    
- Digestive problems
    
- Changes in appetite
    
- Changes in sleep
    
- Changes in sex drive
    
- Moving or speaking more slowly
    

The NHS and NIMH both recognize physical symptoms as part of depression.

This is important because some people seek help for physical symptoms without realizing that their emotional health may also be affected.

However, physical symptoms can have many causes. A persistent headache, stomach problem, fatigue, or other physical complaint should not automatically be attributed to depression.

---

# 13. Feeling Emotionally Numb

Depression is not always intense sadness.

Some people describe feeling:

- Numb
    
- Empty
    
- Detached
    
- Disconnected
    
- Like they are simply going through the motions
    
- Unable to feel excitement or happiness
    

You may not cry or feel visibly upset.

Instead, you may feel as though your emotions have become muted.

This can make depression particularly difficult to recognize because you might think:

**"Nothing is seriously wrong. I just don't feel much anymore."**

That change in emotional experience can still be worth discussing with a professional.

---

# 14. Changes in Social, Work, or School Performance

Depression can affect how you function in everyday life.

You may notice:

- Falling behind at work
    
- Missing classes
    
- Difficulty completing assignments
    
- Making more mistakes
    
- Avoiding responsibilities
    
- Struggling to maintain relationships
    
- Neglecting hobbies
    
- Finding normal routines increasingly difficult
    

The NHS notes that depression can interfere with work, family life, social life, hobbies, and relationships.

Sometimes these changes are noticed by other people before the person experiencing depression recognizes them.

---

# 15. Thoughts About Death or Suicide

Depression can sometimes involve thoughts about death, dying, suicide, or self-harm.

These thoughts may range from:

- "I wish I could disappear."
    
- "I don't want to wake up."
    
- "Everyone would be better without me."
    

to active thoughts about suicide or self-harm.

**These thoughts should always be taken seriously.**

If you are thinking about suicide or hurting yourself, you do not need to manage those thoughts alone. Reach out to someone you trust and seek immediate professional or emergency support.

If you are in India, you can contact **Tele-MANAS at 14416 or 1800-89-14416** for 24/7 mental-health support.

If you are in immediate danger or believe you may act on suicidal thoughts, seek emergency medical care or contact your local emergency service now.

# Do You Need to Have Every Symptom to Have Depression?

**No.**

Depression looks different from person to person.

Some people experience many symptoms, while others experience only a smaller group of symptoms. The severity, frequency, and duration can also vary.

For major depression, healthcare professionals consider the overall pattern of symptoms, including whether symptoms occur most of the day, nearly every day, for at least two weeks. Depressed mood or loss of interest or pleasure is a key part of that diagnostic pattern.

However, you do not need to wait two weeks to ask for help.

If you are struggling now, talking to someone can be appropriate even if you are not sure whether your symptoms meet the criteria for depression.

---

# How Is Depression Different From Normal Sadness?

Feeling sad does not automatically mean you are depressed.

Sadness can happen after:

- A breakup
    
- Losing someone
    
- An argument
    
- Financial problems
    
- Work difficulties
    
- Disappointment
    
- Failure
    
- Major life changes
    

These experiences can be painful without necessarily being depression.

A low mood may improve as you adjust to a situation or as circumstances change.

Depression tends to involve a broader and more persistent pattern that affects daily functioning. The NHS describes depression as more than simply feeling unhappy or fed up for a few days.

Grief can also look similar to depression, and sometimes grief can develop into depression. If you are unsure what you are experiencing, a professional can help you understand the difference.

---

# Can Depression Symptoms Be Physical?

**Yes. Depression can cause physical symptoms as well as emotional symptoms.**

Some people experience fatigue, sleep changes, appetite changes, headaches, body aches, digestive problems, or changes in sex drive.

This does not mean that every physical symptom is caused by depression.

For example, ongoing fatigue could be related to sleep problems, anemia, thyroid problems, medications, infections, or many other causes.

If a physical symptom is persistent or unexplained, consider talking with a healthcare professional.

---

# Can Depression and Anxiety Happen Together?

**Yes.**

Depression and anxiety can occur at the same time.

Someone might experience:

- Persistent sadness
    
- Excessive worry
    
- Loss of interest
    
- Fatigue
    
- Sleep problems
    
- Difficulty concentrating
    
- Irritability
    
- Physical tension
    
- Feelings of hopelessness
    

Because some symptoms overlap, it can be difficult to identify what is happening based on one symptom.

A professional assessment can look at the complete pattern rather than trying to fit yourself into one category.

---

# How Long Do Depression Symptoms Last?

There is no single timeline for every type of depression.

For **major depressive disorder**, clinicians generally look for symptoms occurring most of the day, nearly every day, for at least two weeks, with depressed mood or loss of interest or pleasure being a key symptom.

Other depressive conditions can have different patterns and durations.

If symptoms have been affecting you for several weeks, are getting worse, or are interfering with everyday life, consider seeking professional help.

And again, **you do not have to wait until two weeks have passed to ask for support**.

---

# How Is Depression Diagnosed?

Depression cannot be diagnosed accurately from an online checklist alone.

A doctor, psychologist, psychiatrist, or other qualified healthcare professional may ask about:

- Your mood
    
- Your interests
    
- Sleep
    
- Appetite
    
- Energy
    
- Concentration
    
- Relationships
    
- Work or school
    
- How long symptoms have been present
    
- How symptoms affect your daily life
    
- Thoughts about death or self-harm
    
- Previous mental-health experiences
    

A healthcare professional may also consider whether a physical health condition or medication could be contributing to your symptoms.

Some medical conditions, including thyroid disorders, can produce symptoms that resemble depression.

This is why an assessment can be more useful than trying to diagnose yourself from a symptom list.

---

# When Should I See a Doctor or Mental-Health Professional?

Consider talking with a healthcare professional if:

- Symptoms persist for around two weeks or longer
    
- You have lost interest in things you normally enjoy
    
- You feel persistently hopeless
    
- Your energy has significantly decreased
    
- Your sleep or appetite has changed
    
- You are struggling to concentrate
    
- You are withdrawing from people
    
- Work, school, or relationships are being affected
    
- Everyday tasks are becoming difficult
    
- You are experiencing unexplained physical symptoms
    
- You are unsure whether you may be depressed
    

You can start with a primary-care doctor, psychologist, psychiatrist, or qualified therapist.

You do not need to know whether you have depression before making an appointment.

# What Can You Do If You Recognize These Symptoms?

If you recognize several symptoms in yourself, try not to judge yourself for them.

A few supportive steps may include:

### Talk to someone you trust

Tell a friend, family member, partner, doctor, or mental-health professional what you have been experiencing.

### Keep a basic routine

Try to maintain regular sleep, meals, personal care, and daily responsibilities where possible.

### Stay connected

Depression can make isolation tempting. Even a short conversation or brief time with someone you trust can help you remain connected.

### Get some movement

Gentle physical activity, such as walking, stretching, or another manageable activity, may support overall wellbeing.

### Take small steps

If a large task feels impossible, break it into smaller pieces.

Instead of "I need to clean my entire room," start with one small area.

### Seek professional support

If symptoms persist, worsen, or significantly affect your life, professional support is important.

These strategies can support wellbeing, but they are not a substitute for appropriate medical or mental-health care when depression is significant.

# When Should Depression Be Treated as an Emergency?

Most depressive symptoms do not require emergency treatment, but **immediate help is important when someone may be in danger**.

Seek urgent help if you or someone else:

- Is thinking about suicide
    
- Has a plan to die by suicide
    
- Has recently attempted suicide
    
- Is at immediate risk of self-harm
    
- Cannot stay safe
    

Do not leave someone alone if you believe they are in immediate danger.

Contact local emergency services or go to the nearest emergency department.

In India, **Tele-MANAS (14416 or 1800-89-14416)** provides 24/7 tele-mental-health support.

If you are outside India, contact your country's emergency service or crisis support line.

# The Bottom Line

So, **what are the symptoms of depression?**

Depression can affect much more than your mood. It may cause persistent sadness or emptiness, loss of interest, hopelessness, guilt, low energy, sleep and appetite changes, difficulty concentrating, social withdrawal, irritability, and physical symptoms such as aches, headaches, or digestive problems.

You do not have to experience every symptom, and one or two symptoms do not automatically mean you have depression.

What matters is the **overall pattern, how long it has been happening, how severe it is, and how much it is affecting your life**.

If you recognize several symptoms in yourself, especially if they are persistent or getting worse, consider talking with a healthcare professional.

Depression is a real health condition, and support and effective treatments are available.

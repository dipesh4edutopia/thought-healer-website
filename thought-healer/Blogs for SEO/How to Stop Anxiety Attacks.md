# How to Stop Anxiety Attacks: What to Do When Anxiety Feels Overwhelming

If you are having an intense anxiety episode right now, start by getting somewhere safe and comfortable if possible. Slow your breathing without forcing it, relax your shoulders and jaw, and bring your attention to what is happening around you. Remind yourself that intense anxiety can rise and then settle. You do not have to fight every sensation. If symptoms are new, severe, unusual, or could indicate a medical emergency, seek urgent medical care rather than assuming they are anxiety.

> **How do you stop an anxiety attack?**  
> You cannot always stop an anxiety attack instantly, but you can help your body settle. Move somewhere safe, slow your breathing, relax tense muscles, ground yourself in the present moment, and remind yourself that the episode can pass. Staying connected with someone you trust may also help. If attacks keep happening or interfere with daily life, professional treatment can help.

## What does “anxiety attack” mean?

“Anxiety attack” is a common everyday phrase, but it is not a specific clinical diagnosis. When people describe a sudden episode of overwhelming fear accompanied by physical symptoms such as a racing heart, dizziness, trembling, or shortness of breath, they may be describing a **panic attack**.

Anxiety itself can also build gradually and last much longer. Not every episode of intense anxiety is a panic attack, and experiencing a panic attack does not automatically mean that someone has panic disorder.

The advice below can be useful for intense anxiety and panic-like episodes, but it cannot determine what is causing your symptoms.

## What should I do during an anxiety attack?

When anxiety becomes overwhelming, it can be difficult to remember complicated instructions. Keep the following sequence simple.

### 1. Pause and get somewhere safe

If possible, stop what you are doing and sit or stand somewhere comfortable and safe.

If you are driving, operating machinery, or doing something that requires full attention, stop safely rather than trying to push through intense symptoms.

You do not necessarily need to escape the situation immediately. If you are already somewhere safe, staying there and allowing the sensations to settle may be more helpful than urgently trying to get away.

### 2. Remind yourself that the feeling can pass

During a panic-like episode, physical sensations can feel frightening. A racing heartbeat may make you think something is seriously wrong. Dizziness can make you fear that you will faint. Shortness of breath can make you feel as though you cannot breathe.

Try a simple reminder:

**“This is an intense feeling. I can slow down and let my body settle.”**

This is not about pretending nothing is wrong. It is about avoiding an automatic worst-case interpretation of every sensation.

### 3. Slow your breathing

Rapid breathing can accompany anxiety and may make sensations such as light-headedness or tingling feel more noticeable.

Instead of taking very large breaths, aim for **slow, comfortable breathing**. Let the breath be gentle rather than forceful. You can breathe in slowly and allow a relaxed, slightly longer exhale.

There is no need to breathe as deeply as possible. If focusing on your breathing makes you more anxious, shift your attention toward grounding instead.

The NHS recommends slow breathing during a panic attack and reminding yourself that the episode will pass.

### 4. Bring your attention back to the present

Anxiety often pulls attention toward frightening predictions:

- “What if I lose control?”
    
- “What if something terrible happens?”
    
- “What if this never stops?”
    

Instead, notice what is actually happening around you.

Look at the room. Notice the chair beneath you. Feel your feet against the floor. Listen for sounds nearby.

The goal is not to force anxiety away. It is to give your attention something concrete and present to focus on.

### 5. Relax your body

Anxiety can cause muscles to tense without you realizing it.

Lower your shoulders. Unclench your jaw. Loosen your hands. Let your forehead soften.

You can slowly move through your body and release areas that feel tense. Do not force a deep relaxation response. Even a small reduction in physical tension can make you feel more comfortable.

### 6. Reduce stimulation

If possible, move away from excessive noise, crowds, bright screens, or other stimulation.

A quieter environment may make it easier to concentrate on your breathing and surroundings.

### 7. Connect with someone you trust

If being alone makes the episode harder to manage, contact someone you trust.

You might simply say:

**“I'm feeling very anxious right now. Can you stay with me for a few minutes?”**

You do not have to explain everything while you are distressed.

### 8. Give the episode time to settle

One of the hardest parts of an anxiety or panic episode is the urge to make it stop immediately.

Try to shift the goal from **“I must stop this right now”** to **“I can help myself through this while it settles.”**

That small change can reduce the additional fear created by fighting every sensation.

These strategies do not guarantee that an episode will end immediately. They are intended to reduce distress and help your body and attention settle while the episode passes.

## How long does an anxiety or panic attack last?

The duration varies.

Panic attacks often begin suddenly and symptoms commonly reach their strongest point within minutes before gradually easing. Some symptoms can continue for longer, and the emotional after-effects may remain after the most intense part has passed. NIMH notes that panic attacks can last from a few minutes to an hour or sometimes longer.

Anxiety is different. Instead of arriving as a short, intense wave, anxiety can build gradually and remain present for hours or longer.

If an episode seems unusually long, severe, or different from what you have experienced before, do not automatically assume that it is anxiety.

## What does an anxiety or panic attack feel like?

Panic and intense anxiety can affect both the body and the mind.

### Physical symptoms

Common physical symptoms can include:

- Racing or pounding heartbeat
    
- Sweating
    
- Trembling or shaking
    
- Dizziness or light-headedness
    
- Shortness of breath or rapid breathing
    
- Chest discomfort
    
- Nausea or stomach discomfort
    
- Tingling or numbness
    
- Chills or hot sensations
    
- Feeling weak or faint
    

### Emotional symptoms

You may also experience:

- Intense fear
    
- A sense that something terrible is about to happen
    
- Fear of losing control
    
- Fear of dying
    
- Feeling detached from yourself or your surroundings
    
- Feeling that things are unreal
    
- A strong urge to escape
    

These symptoms can be extremely frightening. However, similar physical symptoms can occur with medical conditions and substance-related effects. That is why severe or unfamiliar symptoms should not automatically be labelled as anxiety.

## Why do anxiety attacks happen?

Anxiety and panic involve the body's threat-response system.

When your brain detects danger, your body can prepare to respond by increasing heart rate, changing breathing, increasing muscle tension, and directing attention toward potential threats.

During a panic attack, this response can become highly activated even when there is no obvious immediate danger. Researchers sometimes describe this as a kind of false alarm in the body's threat system.

Possible contributors include:

- Ongoing stress
    
- Major life changes or difficult events
    
- Accumulated emotional stress
    
- Fear of particular situations
    
- Health-related worries
    
- Lack of sleep
    
- Excessive caffeine
    
- Other stimulants
    
- Certain substances
    
- Some physical health conditions
    
- A tendency to become highly sensitive to physical sensations
    

Sometimes an attack appears to happen **without an obvious trigger**. That does not mean there is necessarily no contributing factor; it simply means you may not be able to identify one in the moment. Panic attacks can occur unexpectedly and, for some people, seemingly out of nowhere.

## How can I calm an anxiety attack quickly?

There is **no guaranteed instant method** for stopping a panic or anxiety attack.

If you are searching for how to stop a panic attack quickly, focus instead on helping your nervous system settle.

Try:

1. **Slow, comfortable breathing** rather than rapid or forceful breathing.
    
2. **Grounding** by noticing sights, sounds, physical sensations, and objects around you.
    
3. **Relaxing tense muscles**, especially your shoulders, jaw, hands, and face.
    
4. **Using realistic self-talk**, such as “I can get through this moment.”
    
5. **Moving somewhere quieter or safer** if your environment is overwhelming.
    
6. **Talking with someone supportive** if connection helps you feel safer.
    
7. **Allowing the sensations to rise and fall** rather than repeatedly checking whether they have disappeared.
    

The aim is not to guarantee that the attack disappears immediately. The aim is to reduce the cycle of fear that can make the experience feel even more intense.

## A simple grounding exercise for anxiety

One simple approach is to use your senses to reconnect with your surroundings.

Try this:

**Look around and identify:**

- 5 things you can see
    
- 4 things you can physically feel
    
- 3 things you can hear
    
- 2 things you can smell
    
- 1 thing you can taste, or simply notice in your mouth
    

Go slowly. You do not have to perform the exercise perfectly.

For example, you might notice the floor beneath your feet, the texture of your clothing, the sound of a fan, the light coming through a window, and the temperature of the room.

Grounding does not “switch off” anxiety. Instead, focusing attention on concrete details may help reduce the amount of attention given to frightening thoughts and bodily sensations.

## What should I avoid during an anxiety attack?

Some understandable reactions can accidentally keep the fear cycle going.

### Repeatedly checking your body

Constantly checking your pulse, breathing, chest sensations, or dizziness can make you more focused on physical symptoms.

If you have already established that your symptoms are not an emergency, try shifting your attention outward.

### Catastrophizing

Thoughts such as “I'm definitely going to die” or “I will completely lose control” can intensify fear.

You do not have to replace them with unrealistic positivity. Try a more balanced thought:

**“This feels frightening, but I don't know that the worst-case scenario is happening.”**

### Using alcohol or recreational drugs

Using substances to escape anxiety can create additional problems and may worsen anxiety for some people. Alcohol and substance misuse can also be associated with panic disorder.

### Excessive caffeine or stimulants

Caffeine and other stimulants can increase physical sensations such as a racing heart or jitteriness. If you notice a connection between stimulants and anxiety, reducing excessive intake may be worth discussing with a healthcare professional.

### Repeatedly searching symptoms online while panicking

Searching every sensation during an episode can provide temporary reassurance but may also keep your attention locked onto frightening possibilities.

If you are experiencing new or severe symptoms, seek appropriate medical advice rather than relying on repeated online searches.

## How can I prevent anxiety attacks from happening again?

There is no guaranteed way to prevent every panic or anxiety episode. However, several habits can support better anxiety management.

### Prioritize consistent sleep

Poor or irregular sleep can make it harder to cope with stress. Aim for a consistent sleep routine where possible.

### Exercise regularly

Regular physical activity can support overall mental health and may help with anxiety management.

### Reduce excessive caffeine

If coffee, energy drinks, or other caffeinated products seem to increase your anxiety or physical symptoms, consider reducing your intake.

### Eat regularly

Skipping meals can make some people feel physically uncomfortable or shaky, which may be confused with or contribute to anxiety sensations.

### Identify patterns

Keep a simple record of when episodes happen:

- What was happening beforehand?
    
- How much sleep had you had?
    
- Had you consumed caffeine?
    
- Were you under unusual stress?
    
- What thoughts appeared?
    
- What helped?
    

The goal is not to monitor yourself obsessively. It is to notice useful patterns.

### Stay connected

Talking with trusted friends, family members, or other supportive people can reduce the sense that you have to manage everything alone.

### Consider professional support

If attacks continue, become more intense, or begin affecting your life, self-help strategies may not be enough. Professional treatment can make a significant difference.

Healthy habits can support mental health, but they are not a substitute for treatment when someone needs more comprehensive care.

## Can therapy help with panic attacks?

Yes. Psychotherapy is an established treatment option for recurrent panic attacks and panic disorder.

**Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT)** is particularly well studied. CBT can help people understand the relationship between physical sensations, thoughts, fear, and avoidance.

For example, someone might notice a racing heartbeat, interpret it as a sign of imminent danger, become more frightened, and then experience even stronger physical symptoms. Therapy can help a person learn different ways of responding to those sensations and thoughts.

CBT may also involve gradually confronting feared sensations or situations in a safe, structured way. This can help reduce avoidance and the fear associated with panic symptoms.

A psychologist, therapist, psychiatrist, primary-care doctor, or other qualified healthcare professional can help determine what type of support is appropriate.

Medication is also sometimes used to treat panic disorder, but whether medication is appropriate depends on the individual's health, symptoms, preferences, and medical history. Medication decisions should be made with a qualified healthcare professional.

## When should I see a doctor or mental-health professional?

Consider professional help if:

- Panic-like episodes happen repeatedly.
    
- You constantly worry about having another attack.
    
- You start avoiding places, people, travel, work, school, or activities.
    
- Anxiety affects your relationships or sleep.
    
- Anxiety interferes with work or studies.
    
- Symptoms are becoming more frequent or severe.
    
- Self-help strategies are not enough.
    
- You are unsure whether your symptoms are caused by anxiety.
    

A healthcare professional can assess your symptoms and consider whether a physical health condition, medication, caffeine, alcohol, or another substance could be contributing.

Importantly, **having a panic attack does not automatically mean you have panic disorder**. Panic disorder involves recurrent, unexpected attacks along with ongoing concern or significant behavioral changes related to future attacks.

## When is an anxiety attack an emergency?

Do not automatically assume severe physical symptoms are anxiety.

Seek **urgent medical care or local emergency services** if you experience symptoms such as:

- New or severe chest pain
    
- Severe difficulty breathing
    
- Fainting or loss of consciousness
    
- Severe or unusual physical symptoms
    
- Symptoms that are substantially different from previous episodes
    
- Symptoms that make you concerned you may be experiencing a medical emergency
    

Panic attacks can cause chest discomfort, breathing difficulty, dizziness, and a racing heart, but these symptoms can overlap with other medical conditions. If you are unsure what is causing them, it is safer to seek medical evaluation.

If you are in immediate danger or experiencing thoughts of suicide or self-harm, seek emergency help now or contact a local crisis service. If possible, stay with someone you trust and do not manage an immediate safety crisis alone.

## Anxiety attack vs. panic attack: What's the difference?

|Experience|What it generally means|
|---|---|
|**Everyday anxiety**|A normal response to stress, uncertainty, or a perceived threat. It may build gradually and can last for some time.|
|**Intense anxiety**|Anxiety can become overwhelming and cause significant physical and emotional symptoms without necessarily fitting the pattern of a panic attack.|
|**Panic attack**|A sudden episode of intense fear or discomfort, often accompanied by strong physical symptoms. It can happen unexpectedly.|
|**Panic disorder**|A mental health condition involving recurrent, unexpected panic attacks together with ongoing worry or behavioral changes related to future attacks.|

These categories can overlap, and only a qualified healthcare professional can determine whether someone's symptoms meet criteria for a specific condition.

# Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I stop an anxiety attack quickly?

There is no guaranteed instant way to stop one. Try slowing your breathing, grounding yourself in the present, relaxing tense muscles, moving somewhere safe and quiet, and reminding yourself that the episode can pass.

### How do I calm myself during an anxiety attack?

Focus on one thing at a time. Relax your shoulders and jaw, breathe slowly and comfortably, notice your surroundings, and use calm, realistic self-talk. If possible, talk with someone you trust.

### What should I do during a panic attack?

Stay somewhere safe if possible, slow your breathing, focus on your surroundings, relax your body, and remind yourself that the intense sensations can settle. Avoid making rapid decisions based on catastrophic thoughts.

### Can deep breathing help an anxiety attack?

Controlled, comfortable breathing may help some people feel calmer. Avoid forcing very deep or rapid breaths. If concentrating on breathing increases your anxiety, try grounding your attention in your surroundings instead.

### How long does an anxiety attack last?

It varies. Panic attacks often reach their strongest point within minutes and then begin to settle, although some symptoms can last longer. Anxiety itself may persist for much longer.

### Why do anxiety attacks happen suddenly?

Panic can involve a strong activation of the body's threat-response system even when there is no obvious danger. Stress, biological factors, environmental factors, and sensitivity to physical sensations may contribute.

### Can anxiety attacks happen for no reason?

Yes. Some panic attacks happen unexpectedly and without an obvious trigger. Not identifying a trigger does not mean the experience is imaginary or that you are doing something wrong.

### How can I prevent anxiety attacks?

There is no guaranteed prevention method. Consistent sleep, regular physical activity, regular meals, reducing excessive caffeine, managing stress, recognizing patterns, and getting appropriate professional support may help reduce symptoms or their impact.

### Should I go to the hospital for a panic attack?

If you are unsure whether symptoms are panic or a medical emergency, seek medical care. New or severe chest pain, severe breathing difficulty, fainting, or unusual symptoms should not automatically be attributed to anxiety.

### When should I seek professional help for anxiety attacks?

Seek professional help when attacks recur, anxiety affects everyday life, you begin avoiding activities or places, symptoms are worsening, or self-help strategies are not enough. A healthcare professional can also check for possible physical or substance-related causes.

## A final word

An anxiety or panic episode can feel overwhelming, especially when physical symptoms make you fear that something is seriously wrong. You do not have to make the feeling disappear instantly.

For the moment, focus on staying safe, slowing down, grounding yourself, relaxing your body, and allowing the intensity to settle. If these episodes keep returning or begin changing how you live your life, reaching out to a qualified healthcare professional is not an overreaction—it is a reasonable next step.

You deserve support, and effective treatments are available for people who struggle with recurring panic and anxiety.

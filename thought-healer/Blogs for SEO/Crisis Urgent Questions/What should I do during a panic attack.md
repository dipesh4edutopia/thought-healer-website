# What Should I Do During a Panic Attack?

A panic attack can feel terrifying. Your heart may race, your chest may feel tight, you may struggle to catch your breath, feel dizzy or shaky, sweat, or suddenly become convinced that something is seriously wrong.

The important thing to know is that **panic attacks are frightening, but the sensations themselves are often temporary**. The safest approach is to slow down, move somewhere safe, focus on your breathing without forcing it, and remind yourself that the intense wave of fear can pass.

However, symptoms such as chest pain, severe difficulty breathing, fainting, or other unusual symptoms can have medical causes too. **If you are unsure whether what you're experiencing is a panic attack—especially if symptoms are severe, new, or different from usual—seek urgent medical evaluation rather than assuming it is anxiety.**

If panic attacks keep happening, become difficult to manage, or cause you to avoid places and activities, consider speaking with a doctor or mental-health professional. Panic disorder and other anxiety conditions are treatable. ([nimh.nih.gov](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/panic-disorder-when-fear-overwhelms?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

# What Should I Do During a Panic Attack Right Now?

When panic hits, your first instinct may be to escape or fight the sensations.

Instead, if you're in a safe environment, try to **pause and let the wave of panic pass while keeping yourself physically safe**.

## 1. Get somewhere safe

If you're driving, operating machinery, standing somewhere dangerous, or in another situation where panic could put you at risk, prioritize physical safety.

If possible, sit down somewhere comfortable.

You don't necessarily need to run away from the situation.

If the environment itself is safe, staying there can help you learn that the panic sensations can rise and fall without requiring immediate escape.

---

## 2. Remind yourself what is happening

Try telling yourself:

> **“This feels frightening, but it may be a panic attack. I can get through this moment.”**

You don't need to convince yourself that nothing is wrong.

Simply remind yourself that intense physical sensations can occur during panic and that the feeling can change.

Panic attacks can involve sudden intense fear or discomfort along with physical and cognitive symptoms such as a racing heart, sweating, trembling, dizziness, shortness of breath, chest discomfort, and fear of losing control. ([nimh.nih.gov](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/panic-disorder-when-fear-overwhelms?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

---

# 3. Breathe Slowly and Gently

Breathing can become fast or irregular during panic.

Rather than taking repeated huge breaths, try to make your breathing **slower and gentler**.

For example:

- breathe in comfortably
    
- breathe out slowly
    
- allow the next breath to happen naturally
    
- avoid forcing your lungs to fill completely
    
- continue at a comfortable pace
    

The goal is not to achieve a “perfect” breathing pattern.

If concentrating on breathing makes you more anxious, stop focusing on it and use another grounding strategy.

---

# 4. Ground Yourself in the Present

Panic can make you intensely focused on internal sensations.

Grounding can shift some of your attention toward your immediate surroundings.

Try noticing:

- five things you can see
    
- four things you can physically feel
    
- three things you can hear
    
- two things you can smell
    
- one thing you can taste
    

You can also simply describe your surroundings:

> “I'm sitting in my room. My feet are on the floor. I can hear traffic outside. The chair is supporting my back.”

The goal isn't to force the panic away.

It's to remind your brain that you are **here, in the present moment**.

---

# 5. Don't Fight Every Sensation

Panic can create a cycle:

**Sensation → frightening interpretation → more fear → stronger sensation → even more fear.**

For example:

> “My heart is racing.”

can become:

> “Something must be terribly wrong.”

which can increase fear and make your heart race even more.

Instead, try:

> “My heart is racing. I'm noticing the sensation. I don't have to solve it immediately.”

This can reduce the additional fear that comes from interpreting every physical sensation as a catastrophe.

---

# 6. Avoid Constantly Checking Your Body

During panic, you may repeatedly check:

- your pulse
    
- your breathing
    
- your heart
    
- your blood pressure
    
- your oxygen level
    
- whether you're dizzy
    
- whether your chest still hurts
    

Understandably, you're looking for reassurance.

But repeated checking can sometimes keep your attention locked onto bodily sensations and reinforce anxiety.

If you have already been medically evaluated and know your symptoms are part of panic, try redirecting your attention rather than repeatedly monitoring every sensation.

However, **don't ignore genuinely new or severe symptoms just because you've experienced panic before**.

---

# 7. Stay Where You Are If It's Safe

Many people instinctively want to escape when panic begins.

Sometimes leaving is necessary for safety.

But if you're somewhere safe, immediately escaping every situation can reinforce the belief:

> “I couldn't cope unless I got out.”

Over time, this can contribute to avoidance.

For people with panic disorder, CBT commonly includes exposure-based strategies that help them gradually face feared sensations or situations rather than continually avoiding them. ([nimh.nih.gov](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/panic-disorder-when-fear-overwhelms?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

You don't have to force yourself into overwhelming situations alone.

A therapist can help you approach feared situations gradually and safely.

---

# 8. Give Yourself Time

A panic attack can feel as though it will continue forever.

But panic symptoms generally rise and fall rather than staying at maximum intensity indefinitely.

Try not to demand:

> “I need this to stop right now.”

Instead:

> “I'm going to get through the next few minutes.”

Breaking the experience into smaller periods can make it feel more manageable.

---

# 9. Talk to Someone You Trust

If having another person nearby makes you feel safer, contact someone you trust.

You can say:

> “I'm having a panic attack. Can you stay with me for a few minutes?”

You don't necessarily need them to fix anything.

Sometimes simply knowing someone is present can make the experience easier to tolerate.

---

# 10. Avoid Making Major Decisions During Panic

When your nervous system is highly activated, everything can feel more urgent.

Try not to make major decisions based solely on what you're feeling in the peak of panic.

First focus on getting somewhere safe and allowing the intensity to decrease.

You can evaluate the situation afterward when you're calmer.

# What Does a Panic Attack Feel Like?

A panic attack is a sudden episode of intense fear or discomfort that can occur unexpectedly or in response to a trigger.

Symptoms can include:

- racing or pounding heart
    
- sweating
    
- trembling
    
- chills or hot sensations
    
- shortness of breath
    
- feeling smothered
    
- chest discomfort
    
- nausea or stomach discomfort
    
- dizziness or lightheadedness
    
- numbness or tingling
    
- feeling detached from yourself or surroundings
    
- fear of losing control
    
- fear of dying
    

Not everyone experiences every symptom.

The experience can also vary from one attack to another.

NIMH describes panic attacks as sudden periods of intense fear or discomfort and notes that physical symptoms can be very strong. ([nimh.nih.gov](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/panic-disorder-when-fear-overwhelms?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

---

# Why Does a Panic Attack Feel So Dangerous?

Panic activates the body's threat-response system.

Your body can respond as though you are facing immediate danger even when there is no obvious external threat.

That can produce:

- increased heart rate
    
- faster breathing
    
- muscle tension
    
- sweating
    
- heightened alertness
    
- intense fear
    

Then you may notice those sensations and interpret them as evidence that something terrible is happening.

That interpretation can produce even more fear.

This creates a feedback loop:

**Fear → physical sensations → frightening interpretation → more fear.**

Understanding this cycle can make the sensations less mysterious.

---

# How Long Does a Panic Attack Last?

The most intense part of a panic attack usually does not last indefinitely.

Some attacks peak relatively quickly and then gradually decrease.

However, you may continue feeling shaken, tired, tense, or anxious after the main wave has passed.

NIMH notes that panic attacks can happen unexpectedly and that fear of another attack can lead people to change their behavior or avoid situations. ([nimh.nih.gov](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/panic-disorder-when-fear-overwhelms?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

# What Should I Do After a Panic Attack?

Once the immediate intensity has decreased, give yourself some time to recover.

You may feel:

- tired
    
- emotionally drained
    
- embarrassed
    
- shaky
    
- frustrated
    
- afraid another attack will happen
    
- unusually alert to physical sensations
    

Try not to criticize yourself.

Instead, consider asking:

### What happened before the attack?

Were you:

- under unusual stress?
    
- sleeping poorly?
    
- consuming a lot of caffeine?
    
- dealing with conflict?
    
- worrying about a particular situation?
    
- in a crowded or unfamiliar place?
    
- experiencing physical symptoms that frightened you?
    

There may not always be an obvious trigger.

Write down what you remember if attacks are recurring. This information can be useful when speaking with a healthcare professional.

---

# Should I Avoid Caffeine During Panic Attacks?

If caffeine seems to worsen your anxiety or trigger physical sensations that you interpret as threatening, reducing your intake may be worth discussing with a healthcare professional.

Caffeine can produce sensations such as a faster heartbeat or jitteriness, which can sometimes be mistaken for anxiety or intensify existing anxiety.

Don't make dramatic changes based on one experience alone. Pay attention to patterns.

---

# Should I Lie Down During a Panic Attack?

If you're dizzy or feel faint, sitting somewhere safe may be appropriate.

You don't necessarily need to lie down.

The most important thing is to prevent falls or injuries and choose a position in which you feel physically safe.

If you actually faint, have severe chest pain, or experience symptoms that don't fit your usual panic attacks, seek medical evaluation.

# Panic Attack vs. Medical Emergency

Panic attacks can produce symptoms that resemble physical illness.

For example, chest discomfort, shortness of breath, dizziness, and a racing heart can occur during panic—but they can also occur with medical conditions.

**Do not automatically assume new or severe symptoms are anxiety.**

Seek urgent medical care if:

- chest pain is severe, new, or persistent
    
- you have severe difficulty breathing
    
- you faint or lose consciousness
    
- you develop sudden neurological symptoms
    
- symptoms are substantially different from previous panic attacks
    
- you have a medical condition that makes the symptoms concerning
    
- you believe something may be medically wrong
    

If you are unsure, it is safer to seek medical advice.

A healthcare professional can help determine whether your symptoms are consistent with panic or whether another medical explanation needs to be considered.

# What If I Keep Having Panic Attacks?

An occasional panic attack does not necessarily mean you have panic disorder.

However, repeated unexpected panic attacks followed by persistent worry about having another attack or significant changes in behavior can be associated with **panic disorder**. ([nimh.nih.gov](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/panic-disorder-when-fear-overwhelms?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

For example, you might start:

- avoiding public transportation
    
- avoiding crowded places
    
- avoiding exercise because you're afraid your heart will race
    
- avoiding being alone
    
- repeatedly checking your body
    
- avoiding leaving home
    
- carrying “just in case” items everywhere
    
- needing another person with you
    

When fear of another attack begins controlling your life, professional support can be particularly useful.

# Can Panic Attacks Be Treated?

Yes.

Panic disorder and other anxiety conditions are treatable.

Treatment may involve:

- psychotherapy
    
- CBT
    
- exposure-based approaches
    
- medication
    
- lifestyle and behavioral strategies
    
- a combination of approaches
    

NIMH identifies CBT as a commonly used psychotherapy for panic disorder and describes exposure as one component that can help people gradually confront feared sensations and situations. ([nimh.nih.gov](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/panic-disorder-when-fear-overwhelms?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

A healthcare professional can help determine which approach is appropriate.

---

# Can Medication Help Panic Attacks?

Medication can be useful for some people with panic disorder or other anxiety conditions.

However, medication isn't automatically necessary for everyone who experiences a panic attack.

A psychiatrist or doctor may consider:

- how often attacks occur
    
- their severity
    
- whether you are avoiding situations
    
- other mental-health symptoms
    
- medical history
    
- previous treatment
    
- your preferences
    

Do not start, stop, or change psychiatric medication without professional guidance.

---

# What If I'm Afraid of Having Another Panic Attack?

Fear of another attack can become part of the problem.

You might start thinking:

> “What if it happens again?”

Then you monitor your body for signs of danger.

A small sensation appears.

You become alarmed.

The anxiety increases.

And the sensations become stronger.

This can create a cycle of **fear of fear**.

CBT and exposure-based treatment can help people learn that uncomfortable sensations and anxious thoughts can be experienced without automatically responding with avoidance or escape. ([nimh.nih.gov](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/panic-disorder-when-fear-overwhelms?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

# What Not to Do During a Panic Attack

Try to avoid:

- repeatedly telling yourself you're definitely having a heart attack when you haven't been medically assessed
    
- constantly checking your pulse or symptoms
    
- searching the internet for catastrophic explanations of every sensation
    
- using alcohol or recreational drugs to calm yourself
    
- driving if you're too distressed to drive safely
    
- forcing extremely deep breaths
    
- immediately avoiding every situation associated with panic
    
- criticizing yourself for having the attack
    

At the same time, **don't dismiss genuine medical symptoms as “just panic.”**

If something feels medically different or dangerous, seek appropriate care.

---

# When Should I See a Doctor or Mental-Health Professional?

Consider professional help if:

- panic attacks keep recurring
    
- you worry constantly about another attack
    
- you avoid places because you're afraid of panicking
    
- panic interferes with work or studies
    
- you stop socializing because of fear
    
- you're repeatedly seeking reassurance about your physical health
    
- anxiety is affecting sleep
    
- you're using alcohol or substances to cope
    
- you're not sure whether your symptoms are panic or something physical
    

A doctor can assess possible physical causes, while a psychologist or psychiatrist can evaluate anxiety and panic symptoms and discuss treatment options.

# Frequently Asked Questions

## 1. What should I do during a panic attack?

Move somewhere safe, slow your breathing without forcing it, ground yourself in the present, and remind yourself that the intense wave of fear can pass. If symptoms are new, severe, or different from previous attacks, seek medical evaluation rather than automatically assuming they're panic.

## 2. How can I stop a panic attack quickly?

There is no guaranteed instant way to stop a panic attack. Staying safe, slowing your breathing gently, grounding yourself, and reducing catastrophic interpretations can help you get through the episode. The goal is often to allow the intensity to rise and fall rather than fighting every sensation.

## 3. Should I take deep breaths during a panic attack?

Try breathing slowly and gently rather than repeatedly taking very deep breaths. For some people, forcing deep breaths can increase discomfort. If focusing on breathing makes you more anxious, shift your attention to grounding or your surroundings.

## 4. How long does a panic attack last?

The most intense part of a panic attack usually peaks and then decreases rather than remaining at maximum intensity indefinitely. However, you may feel anxious, tired, or shaken afterward. Recurrent attacks or fear of future attacks may require professional assessment.

## 5. How do I know if it's a panic attack or a heart problem?

You cannot reliably diagnose the cause based only on symptoms. Panic can cause chest discomfort, shortness of breath, dizziness, and a racing heart, but medical conditions can cause similar symptoms. New, severe, persistent, or unusual symptoms warrant medical evaluation.

## 6. Can panic attacks happen for no reason?

Yes. Panic attacks can occur unexpectedly, without an obvious immediate trigger. They can also happen in response to particular situations or feared sensations. Repeated unexpected attacks combined with ongoing worry or avoidance may be associated with panic disorder.

## 7. Can panic attacks go away on their own?

An individual panic attack can subside on its own, but recurring panic attacks should not simply be ignored. If attacks are affecting your daily life or causing avoidance, evidence-based treatments such as CBT can help.

## 8. Can therapy help with panic attacks?

Yes. CBT and exposure-based approaches are commonly used for panic disorder. Therapy can help you understand the panic cycle, change catastrophic interpretations, reduce avoidance, and gradually become less fearful of panic sensations. ([nimh.nih.gov](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/panic-disorder-when-fear-overwhelms?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

## 9. Do I need medication for panic attacks?

Not necessarily. Some people benefit from psychotherapy alone, while others may benefit from medication or a combination of treatments. A healthcare professional can consider your symptoms, medical history, severity, and preferences.

## 10. Should I go to the hospital for a panic attack?

If symptoms are new, severe, medically concerning, or substantially different from previous panic attacks, seek medical care. If you experience severe chest pain, severe difficulty breathing, fainting, or another emergency symptom, seek urgent medical attention rather than assuming it is panic.
